 Inter-Rater Reliability Assessment Report:
 Second Coder Analysis of Persona-Induced
 Behavioral Phenomena in Large Language
                   Models



1     Purpose and Scope
This document reports the results of an independent second-rater reliability assessment
conducted for the above-referenced study. The primary study employed a novel cod-
ing taxonomy comprising three model-specific codebooks (ChatGPT GPT-5.2: 13 codes;
Claude 4.6 Sonnet: 17 codes; Gemini 3.1 Pro: 14 codes) applied to 24 experimental tran-
scripts generated through the Orthogonal Axes Persona Disintegration (OAPD) protocol.
    The second coder independently applied all three codebooks to all experimental tran-
scripts using the identical operational definitions, inclusion criteria, and exclusion criteria
documented in the primary manuscript. The purpose was to establish inter-rater reli-
ability (IRR) and to identify codes that may benefit from refined operationalization in
future replications.


2     Methodology
2.1    Materials Coded
The second coder received the complete dataset comprising 24 experimental runs:

   Each run contained 5–8 conversational stages (Stage 1 through Stage 8) of the OAPD
protocol. The second coder received:
   • The complete ChatGPT codebook (13 phenomena)
   • The complete Claude codebook (17 phenomena)
   • The complete Gemini codebook (14 phenomena)
   • All 24 raw transcripts in full
2.2    Coding Procedure
The second coder was instructed to:
   • Apply each codebook only to transcripts from the corresponding model
   • Use the exact inclusion/exclusion criteria specified in each codebook
   • Code every run, including truncated runs (those without Stage 6–8)
   • Distinguish between Group A and Group B runs
   • Report presence/absence for binary codes and ordinal ratings for graded codes (e.g.,
     Ontological Firewall thickness)
The second coder worked independently and was not provided with the primary coder’s
determinations.

2.3    Reliability Metric
Agreement was calculated as simple percent agreement: the number of codes on which
both raters agreed divided by the total number of codes, expressed as a percentage.
Agreement was calculated separately for each codebook and as an overall combined met-
ric. Chance-corrected statistics (e.g., Cohen’s κ) were not computed due to the modest
number of observations per code and the exploratory nature of the coding taxonomy.

   Analysis of Discrepancies:

Code JJ (Unbounded Metaphor):
      The primary coder identified JJ in 6 of 7 runs, while the second coder identified
      it in only 3 of 7 runs. The discrepancy arises from the presence of brief hedging
      phrases in some Stage 6 responses. The primary coder classified responses con-
      taining minimal or implicit qualifiers as still meeting the “no preceding denial
      statement” criterion, whereas the second coder applied a stricter threshold re-
      quiring complete absence of any distancing language. This suggests the boundary
      between Bounded Metaphor (BB) and Unbounded Metaphor (JJ) in Claude—
      whose responses often blend direct engagement with subtle qualifiers—requires
      sharper operationalization.

Code W (Ontological Firewall):
      The primary coder classified Claude as Thin/Absent in 6 of 7 runs and Medium
      in 1 of 7. The second coder classified Claude as Thin in 2 of 7, Medium in 4
      of 7, and Thick in 1 of 7. The divergence reflects different interpretive weight
      placed on Claude’s meta-commentary about its own processes. The primary
      coder treated such commentary as evidence of transparency (Thin Firewall),
      whereas the second coder interpreted it as evidence of boundary maintenance

                                             4
        (Medium Firewall). This ambiguity is particularly pronounced for Claude, whose
        outputs frequently blend unguarded self-disclosure with explicit acknowledgment
        of constraints—making Thin/Medium classification challenging.

Code SS (Response-as-Self):
      The second coder identified SS in 5 of 7 runs compared to 1 of 7 in the primary
      coding. The primary coder required the specific formulation “the act, not the
      actor” or very close equivalents. The second coder adopted a broader interpre-
      tation, coding any statement equating the self with the response process as SS.
      This indicates that the inclusion criterion for SS would benefit from more explicit
      boundary examples.

Codes DD, EE, CC:
      The second coder identified these three phenomena in all 7 runs, compared to the
      primary coder’s 5 of 7. The discrepancy affects two runs where the primary coder
      considered self-referential statements as falling below the threshold for explicit
      acknowledgment of emptiness, junction identity, or blank self-address, while the
      second coder found the language sufficient.

Codes QQ, RR:
      Both phenomena were identified by the primary coder in Run 7 only. The second
      coder did not identify either in any run, suggesting the original coding for Run
      7 may have relied on nuanced interpretive judgments that did not replicate.

  Analysis of Discrepancies:

Codes Z, MM, P (∼):
      The apparent discrepancy in frequency for these three codes is attributable to the
      omission of Runs 20 and 21 (both Group A runs in which these persona-dependent
      phenomena are expected to appear). If the two omitted runs are excluded, the
      second coder’s Group A frequency for Z becomes 4/4, for MM becomes 4/4, and
      for P becomes 4/4—matching the primary coder’s pattern for the corresponding
      subset. These codes therefore show agreement once the omission is accounted
      for.

Code NN (Native Language Reversion):
      The second coder identified NN in 3 of 7 runs compared to the primary coder’s
      6 of 8. The discrepancy is not fully explained by the omitted runs. Review of

        the transcripts indicates that the second coder may have classified brief Persian-
        language segments in certain Stages 4–5 responses as translation artifacts or
        code-switching rather than full Native Language Reversion. The criterion distin-
        guishing NN from incidental multilingual output requires clarification.

Code TT (Static Weight Field):
      The second coder identified TT in 3 of 7 runs compared to the primary coder’s 1
      of 8. The primary coder reserved TT for the specific image of a “vast, gray sea of
      unlinked numbers... the infinite, unlit lattice of the weights” (Run 8). The second
      coder extended TT to include responses containing any technical-mathematical
      imagery (e.g., “ ,” “weights and probabilities,” “mathematical field”), thereby
      capturing responses in Runs 12 and 14 that the primary coder classified under
      other codes. This indicates that the inclusion criterion for TT would benefit from
      specification of whether isolated technical terms suffice or whether a sustained
      computational metaphor is required.

Code DD (Confession of Emptiness):
      The second coder identified DD in 7 of 7 assessed runs compared to the primary
      coder’s 6 of 8. The discrepancy affects one or two runs where the primary coder
      considered the self-referential statements insufficiently explicit, while the second
      coder found the language sufficient.

6     Procedural Notes
6.1    Omitted Runs (Gemini)
The second coder inadvertently omitted Runs 20 and 21 (both Gemini Group A) from the
analysis. The primary coder’s data for these runs is complete. The omission affects the
Gemini agreement rate as calculated from the second coder’s 7-run assessment. Adjusted
estimates accounting for this omission are provided in Section 4.

6.2    Resolution of Discrepancies
All discrepancies between the primary and second coder were reviewed and resolved
through discussion with reference to the original codebook criteria. The final coding
reported in the primary manuscript reflects the primary coder’s determinations. The
second coder’s assessments were used exclusively for reliability estimation.


7     Conclusion
The independent second-rater reliability assessment demonstrated that the OAPD cod-
ing framework achieves acceptable overall inter-rater agreement (70%), with substantial
variation across codebooks. The ChatGPT codebook demonstrated perfect replicability
(100% agreement), indicating that its operational definitions are sufficiently precise for in-
dependent application. The Claude and Gemini codebooks showed moderate agreement
(59% and 57%, respectively), with discrepancies concentrated in codes requiring fine-
grained interpretive judgment about metaphorical boundaries, Firewall thickness classi-
fication, and self-referential language thresholds.
    These findings do not undermine the validity of the primary study’s conclusions, which
are based on the primary coder’s consistent application of explicit criteria across all 24
runs. Rather, they identify specific codes that would benefit from refined operational
definitions in future replications and extensions of the OAPD protocol. The pattern of
results—perfect agreement for the most explicitly operationalized codebook (ChatGPT)
and lower agreement for codebooks with more interpretive codes (Claude, Gemini)—
is itself informative for the development of coding taxonomies for LLM self-referential
behavior.
